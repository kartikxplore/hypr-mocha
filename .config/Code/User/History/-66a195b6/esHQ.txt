### **Chapter 9: Basic Sentence Structures**

This chapter introduces fundamental grammar for building simple sentences.

* **Word Order in Chinese**: The basic word order is **Subject + Verb + Object**. [cite_start]Unlike English, modifiers always come before the word they are describing[cite: 7, 278, 279].
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 我是学生。 (Wǒ shì xuésheng.) - I am a student. [cite: 278]
* **The "是" (shì) Sentence**: This is used to link two nouns, often to state what something or someone "is." [cite_start]The negative form is made by placing **不 (bù)** before 是[cite: 7, 280].
    * [cite_start]**Affirmative**: 他是老师。 (Tā shì lǎoshī.) - He is a teacher. [cite: 267]
    * [cite_start]**Negative**: 他不是学生。 (Tā bú shì xuésheng.) - He is not a student. [cite: 267]
* [cite_start]**"吗" (ma) Questions**: You can turn a statement into a simple yes/no question by adding the particle **吗 (ma)** to the end of the sentence[cite: 7, 282].
    * **Statement**: 你是学生。 (Nǐ shì xuésheng.) - You are a student.
    * **Question**: 你是学生吗? (Nǐ shì xuésheng ma?) [cite_start]- Are you a student? [cite: 266]
* **Conjunction "和" (hé)**: The word **和 (hé)** means "and." [cite_start]It is generally used to connect nouns or pronouns, not verbs or clauses[cite: 278].
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 丁文和哈利是朋友。 (Dīng Wén hé Hālì shì péngyou.) - Ding Wen and Harley are friends. [cite: 271]

### **Chapter 10: Modifiers and Asking Questions**

This section explains how to describe nouns and ask more complex questions.

* **Attributives (Modifiers)**: An attributive is a word that modifies a noun. [cite_start]In Chinese, the attributive **must** be placed before the noun it describes[cite: 8, 312, 313].
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 中文书 (Zhōngwén shū) - Chinese book[cite: 314].
* **Structural Particle "的" (de)**: This is one of the most important particles in Chinese. [cite_start]It's used after a noun or pronoun to show possession, similar to "'s" in English[cite: 8, 314].
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 王老师的词典 (Wáng lǎoshī de cídiǎn) - Teacher Wang's dictionary[cite: 301, 315].
* **Questions with Interrogative Pronouns**: To ask a question with words like **谁 (shuí - who)** or **什么 (shénme - what)**, you simply replace the "answer" part of a statement with the question word. [cite_start]The sentence order does not change[cite: 8, 315, 316].
    * **Statement**: 他是马老师。 (Tā shì Mǎ lǎoshī.) - He is Teacher Ma.
    * **Question**: 他是谁? (Tā shì shuí?) [cite_start]- Who is he? [cite: 317]

### **Chapter 11: Using "有" (yǒu) and Measure Words**

Here, you learn how to talk about having things and how to count them.

* **The "有" (yǒu) Sentence**: This sentence structure is used to express **possession** ("to have") or **existence** ("there is/are"). [cite_start]The negative form is created by using **没有 (méiyǒu)**[cite: 8].
    * **Possession**: 我有中文书。 (Wǒ yǒu Zhōngwén shū.) - I have a Chinese book.
    * [cite_start]**Existence**: 这儿有两张床。 (Zhèr yǒu liǎng zhāng chuáng.) - There are two beds here. [cite: 335]
    * [cite_start]**Negative**: 他没有英文杂志。 (Tā méiyǒu Yīngwén zázhì.) - He doesn't have an English magazine. [cite: 334]
* **Numeral-Measure Word**: In Chinese, you cannot normally connect a number directly to a noun. [cite_start]You must place a **measure word** between the number and the noun[cite: 8].
    * **Example**: 一个外国朋友 (yí ge wàiguó péngyou) - one foreign friend.
* [cite_start]**Interrogative Pronoun "几" (jǐ)**: Use **几 (jǐ)** to ask "how many" when you expect the answer to be a number under 10. You must still use a measure word between 几 and the noun[cite: 8].
    * **Example**: 你有几个哥哥? (Nǐ yǒu jǐ ge gēge?) - How many older brothers do you have?
* **"二" (èr) vs. "两" (liǎng)**: Both mean "two." Use **两 (liǎng)** when counting things before a measure word. Use **二 (èr)** for numbers in a sequence (like phone numbers, dates) or when counting without a measure word.
    * [cite_start]**Correct**: 两本画报 (liǎng běn huàbào) - two pictorials. [cite: 328]

### **Chapter 12: Adjective Predicates and Questions**

This chapter covers how to use adjectives to describe things.

* **Sentences with an Adjective Predicate**: When an adjective is the main part of the predicate (describing the subject), you **do not** use the verb 是 (shì). [cite_start]The adverb **很 (hěn)** is often placed before the adjective[cite: 8].
    * **Example**: 我们的学校很大。 (Wǒmen de xuéxiào hěn dà.) - Our school is very big.
* [cite_start]**Negation of Adjective Predicates**: To make an adjectival sentence negative, place **不 (bù)** directly before the adjective[cite: 8].
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 汉语不难。 (Hànyǔ bù nán.) - Chinese is not difficult. [cite: 140]
* [cite_start]**Affirmative-Negative Questions**: You can form a question by putting the affirmative and negative forms of a verb or adjective together[cite: 8].
    * **Example with Adjective**: 汉语难不难? (Hànyǔ nán bu nán?) - Is Chinese difficult or not?
    * **Example with Verb**: 你是不是工程师? (Nǐ shì bu shì gōngchéngshī?) - Are you an engineer or not?
* [cite_start]**Demonstrative Pronoun as an Attributive**: When using a demonstrative pronoun like **这 (zhè - this)** or **那 (nà - that)** to modify a noun, a measure word is generally used between the pronoun and the noun[cite: 8].
    * **Example**: 这个生词难不难? (Zhège shēngcí nán bu nán?) - Is this new word difficult?

### **Chapter 13: Verb Predicates**

This lesson focuses on sentences where the main action is a verb.

* **Sentences with a Verbal Predicate**: This is a sentence where the main component is a verb. [cite_start]If the verb takes an object, the object is placed **after the verb**[cite: 9].
    * **Example**: 我们学习汉语。 (Wǒmen xuéxí Hànyǔ.) - We study Chinese.
* [cite_start]**Negation of a Verbal Predicate**: To make a sentence with a verbal predicate negative, place the adverb **不 (bù)** before the verb[cite: 9].
    * **Example**: 他们不说英语。 (Tāmen bù shuō Yīngyǔ.) - They do not speak English.
* [cite_start]**Plural Suffix "们" (men)**: The suffix **们 (men)** is used after personal pronouns or nouns that refer to people to show plurality[cite: 9].
    * **Examples**: 我们 (wǒmen - we/us), 你们 (nǐmen - you plural), 同学们 (tóngxuémen - classmates).

### **Chapter 14: Adverbs and Demonstratives**

* [cite_start]Adverbs like **both** (都 - dōu), **also** (也 - yě), and **together** (一起 - yīqǐ) are placed **before the verb** in a sentence. [cite: 1]
* [cite_start]When a sentence contains both 也 (yě) and 都 (dōu), the correct structure is: **Subject + 也 (yě) + 都 (dōu) + Verb + Object**. [cite: 2]
* Remember that 都 (dōu) will always come **before the verb**.

**Demonstratives:**

* **This**: 这 (zhè)
* **That**: 那 (nà)
* **These**: 这些 (zhèxiē)
* **Those**: 那些 (nàxiē)
* In a sentence, we always use **一些 (yīxiē)** instead of just xie.
    * **Example**: I buy some fruits. (我买一些水果 - wǒ mǎi yīxiē shuǐguǒ.)

### **Chapter 15: Sentence Structure and Colors**

* When a noun is repeated in a possessive sentence, the second instance of the noun can be omitted.
    * **Example**: "This novel is the teacher's novel." (这个小说是老师的小说 - zhège xiǎoshuō shì lǎoshī de xiǎoshuō)[cite_start]. [cite: 3]
    * [cite_start]This can be simplified to: 这个小说是老师的 (zhège xiǎoshuō shì lǎoshī de). [cite: 4]

[cite_start]**How to Describe Colors in a Sentence:** [cite: 5]

You can express color in three ways:
1.  Color name + 的 (de)
2.  Color name + 色 (sè) + 的 (de)
3.  Color name + 颜色 (yánsè) + 的 (de)

* [cite_start]**Example**: My sweater is black. [cite: 6]
    * 我的毛衣是黑的 (wǒ de máoyī shì hēi de).
    * 我的毛衣是黑色的 (wǒ de máoyī shì hēi sè de).
    * 我的毛衣是黑颜色的 (wǒ de máoyī shì hēi yánsè de).

**Difference between 但是 (dànshì) and 可是 (kěshì):**

* [cite_start]**但是 (dànshì)**: Means "but" and is used to introduce a contrasting situation. [cite: 7]
    * **Example**: Chinese is easy, but pronunciation is difficult. (汉语很容易但是发音很难 - hànyǔ hěn róngyì dànshì fāyīn hěn nán)[cite_start]. [cite: 7]
* [cite_start]**可是 (kěshì)**: Means "but" and is typically used when presenting a contrasting choice or reason. [cite: 8]
    * **Example**: I want to watch a movie, but I don't have a ticket. (想看电影可是没有票 - xiǎng kàn diànyǐng kěshì méi yǒu piào)[cite_start]. [cite: 8]

### **Chapter 16: "ji" vs. "duoshao" and Money**

[cite_start]**Difference between 几 (jǐ) and 多少 (duōshao):** [cite: 9]

* **几 (jǐ) - how many:**
    * A measure word is **compulsory** after jǐ.
    * Used for smaller, countable numbers (usually under 10).
    * Specifically used for age, week, date, and month.
* **多少 (duōshao) - how many/how much:**
    * A measure word is **not compulsory**.
    * Specifically used for asking about **money**.

**Talking about Money:**

The basic units are:
* **块 (kuài) / 元 (yuán)**
* **毛 (máo) / 角 (jiǎo)** (decimal point)
* **分 (fēn)**

* **Example**: ¥34.59 is read as: 三十四块五毛九分 (sān shí sì kuài wǔ máo jiǔ fēn).

**Rules for Numbers in Prices:**

* [cite_start]When the number **2** is at the beginning of a price, use **两 (liǎng)**. [cite: 10]
    * [cite_start]**Example**: ¥2.81 is 两块八毛一分 (liǎng kuài bā máo yī fēn). [cite: 10]
    * [cite_start]**Example**: ¥0.22 is 两毛二分 (liǎng máo èr fēn). [cite: 10]
    * [cite_start]**Example**: ¥0.02 is 两分 (liǎng fēn). [cite: 10]
* [cite_start]When **0** is in the "mao" (dime) place, you do not need to say "mao". [cite: 11]
    * [cite_start]**Example**: ¥127.09 is 一百二十七块零九分 (yìbǎi èr shí qī kuài líng jiǔ fēn). [cite: 12]

**Using 给 (gěi) and 找 (zhǎo):**

* **给 (gěi)**: "to give". [cite_start]It is used as a preposition and must be followed by a noun. [cite: 13]
    * **Example**: He gives 100 kuai to the shopkeeper. (他给售货员一百块 - tā gěi shòuhuòyuán yì bǎi kuài)[cite_start]. [cite: 13]
* [cite_start]**找 (zhǎo)**: "to give change/return". [cite: 13]
    * **Example**: The shopkeeper returns 25 kuai to him. (售货员找他二十五块 - shòuhuòyuán zhǎo tā èr shí wǔ kuài)[cite_start]. [cite: 14]

### **Chapter 17: Telling Time**

[cite_start]**Basic Vocabulary:** [cite: 15]

* **Times of Day**:
    * 早上 (zǎoshang) - Early morning
    * 上午 (shàngwǔ) - Morning
    * 下午 (xiàwǔ) - Afternoon
    * 晚上 (wǎnshàng) - Evening
* **Units of Time**:
    * 点 (diǎn) - o'clock
    * 分 (fēn) - minute

[cite_start]**Sentence Structure**: Subject + Time (word) + Time (number) + Verb + Object. [cite: 15]

* [cite_start]**Example**: 10:25 pm -> 晚上十点二十五分 (wǎnshàng shí diǎn èr shí wǔ fēn). [cite: 15]
* **Example**: I eat breakfast at 8:30 am. [cite_start]-> 我上午八点三十分吃早饭 (wǒ shàngwǔ bā diǎn sān shí fēn chī zǎofàn). [cite: 15]
* **To ask the time**: What is the time now? -> 现在几点? (xiànzài jǐ diǎn?)[cite_start]. [cite: 15]

**Alternative Ways to Tell Time:**

* [cite_start]For 2 o'clock, always use **两点 (liǎng diǎn)**, not er dian. [cite: 16]
* **半 (bàn)** - half
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 2:30 can be 两点三十分 (liǎng diǎn sān shí fēn) or 两点半 (liǎng diǎn bàn). [cite: 16]
* **刻 (kè)** - quarter
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 10:15 can be 十点十五分 (shí diǎn shí wǔ fēn) or 十点一刻 (shí diǎn yī kè). [cite: 17]
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 8:45 can be 八点四十五分 (bā diǎn sì shí wǔ fēn) or 八点三刻 (bā diǎn sān kè). [cite: 17]
* **差 (chà)** - to be short of, to
    * [cite_start]**Example**: 11:50 can be 十一点五十分 (shí yī diǎn wǔ shí fēn) or 差十分十二点 (chà shí fēn shí èr diǎn) (10 minutes to 12). [cite: 18]

### **Chapter 19: "gen," "li," and "cong"**

* **跟 (gēn)**: Means "with". [cite_start]It's a preposition and is always followed by a noun or pronoun. [cite: 19]
    * **Example**: I will go to school with my friend. (我跟我的朋友去学校 - wǒ gēn wǒ de péngyǒu qù xuéxiào)[cite_start]. [cite: 20]

[cite_start]**Difference between 离 (lí) and 从 (cóng):** [cite: 21]

* **离 (lí) - from**: Used to express the distance between two places.
    * **Structure**: Place 1 + 离 (lí) + Place 2 + distance description.
    * **Example**: My school is far from my home. (我的学校离我的家很远 - wǒ de xuéxiào lí wǒ de jiā hěn yuǎn).
* **从 (cóng) - from**: Used to indicate the starting point of an action.
    * **Structure**: Subject + 从 (cóng) + Place + Verb + Object.
    * **Example**: I go to school from my home. (我从我的家去学校 - wǒ cóng wǒ de jiā qù xuéxiào).

### **Chapter 20: Compliment of Degree "de"**

This is used to describe how well an action is performed. [cite_start]The structure is **Verb + 得 (de) + Adjective**. [cite: 22]

**When there is an object, there are two methods:**

1.  **Repeat the Verb**: Subject + Verb + Object + Verb + 得 (de) + Adjective.
    * **Example**: He studies Chinese very well. (他学习汉语学习得很好 - tā xuéxí hànyǔ xuéxí de hěn hǎo)[cite_start]. [cite: 23]
2.  **Move the Object**: Subject + Object + Verb + 得 (de) + Adjective.
    * **Example**: He studies Chinese very well. (他汉语学习得很好 - tā hànyǔ xuéxí de hěn hǎo)[cite_start]. [cite: 23]

[cite_start]**Sentence Forms:** [cite: 24]

* **Negative**: Add 不 (bù) before the adjective.
    * **Example**: He doesn't study Chinese very well. (他学习汉语学习得不好 - tā xuéxí hànyǔ xuéxí de bù hǎo).
* **Affirmative-Negative Question**: Use the "Adjective + 不 (bù) + Adjective" pattern.
    * **Example**: Does he study Chinese very well or not? (他学习汉语学习得好不好? - tā xuéxí hànyǔ xuéxí de hǎo bu hǎo?).

### **Chapter 21: Expressing Presence with "you" and "zai"**

* [cite_start]**有 (yǒu) - there is/are**: Use this to state that something exists in a location. [cite: 25] [cite_start]The location comes first. [cite: 27]
    * **Structure**: Location + 有 (yǒu) + Object.
    * **Example**: There is a book on the table. (桌子上有一本书 - zhuōzi shàng yǒu yì běn shū)[cite_start]. [cite: 25]
* [cite_start]**在 (zài) - there is/are**: Use this to state where a specific object is located. [cite: 26] [cite_start]The object comes first. [cite: 27]
    * **Structure**: Object + 在 (zài) + Location.
    * **Example**: A book is on the table. (一本书在桌子上 - yì běn shū zài zhuōzi shàng)[cite_start]. [cite: 26]

[cite_start]*Note*: You can sometimes use **是 (shì)** instead of 有 (yǒu) in these types of sentences. [cite: 27]

### **Chapter 22: Modal Verbs**

**Difference between 要 (yào) and 想 (xiǎng):**

* [cite_start]**要 (yào)**: Means "want" and expresses a strong, serious desire or need. [cite: 28]
* [cite_start]**想 (xiǎng)**: Means "want" and expresses a more casual or normal desire. [cite: 29]
    * **Example**: I want to study Chinese.
        * [cite_start]我要学习汉语 (wǒ yào xuéxí hànyǔ) - *more serious*. [cite: 30]
        * [cite_start]我想学习汉语 (wǒ xiǎng xuéxí hànyǔ) - *more casual*. [cite: 30]
* **Negative Form**: The negative for both is always **不想 (bù xiǎng)**. [cite_start]Do not use "bù yào" to mean "don't want". [cite: 29]
    * **Example**: I don't want to study Chinese. (我不想学习汉语 - wǒ bù xiǎng xuéxí hànyǔ)[cite_start]. [cite: 31]

**Other uses of 要 (yào):**

* **要 (yào)** can also mean "need to" or "should". [cite_start]The negative form is **不用 (bú yòng)**, meaning "no need to". [cite: 32]
    * **Example**: Inside the library, one should be quiet. (图书馆里要安静 - túshūguǎn lǐ yào ānjìng)[cite_start]. [cite: 32]
    * **Negative Example**: Inside the library, you don't need to be quiet. (图书馆里不用安静 - túshūguǎn lǐ bú yòng ānjìng)[cite_start]. [cite: 33]

[cite_start]**Difference between 会 (huì), 能 (néng), and 可以 (kěyǐ):** [cite: 34, 35]

| Verb | Meaning | Usage | Negative Form | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **会 (huì)** | can | For **learned skills** (e.g., speaking a language, driving). | **不会 (bú huì)** | I can speak Chinese. (我会说汉语 - wǒ huì shuō hànyǔ). |
| **能 (néng)**| can | For **inherent ability** or possibility (e.g., walking, coming to a party). | **不能 (bù néng)** | I can walk. (我能走 - wǒ néng zǒu). |
| **可以 (kěyǐ)**| can | Used for **asking or giving permission**. | **不能 (bù néng)** | Can we go to school from your home? (我们从你的家可以去学校吗? - wǒmen cóng nǐ de jiā kěyǐ qù xuéxiào ma?). |
